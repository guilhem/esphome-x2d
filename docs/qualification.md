# ESP32-S3 qualification

No ESP32-S3/CC1101 waveform or motor qualification has been performed for this implementation. The normal example and CI builds keep RF disabled. Host simulation, code generation and compilation are separate evidence.

## Private trial authorization

Use the existing supervised Well'com frame hypothesis: a fresh nonzero 16-bit prefix plus the privately observed identity suffix, initial counter zero, 24 copies per enrollment phase, and a 2001 ms start-to-start separation. The lifecycle journal allocates prefixes monotonically from a random seed. Commands use 25 copies. These parameters do not establish general motor compatibility; the core's public candidate suffix `0x01` is hardware-unqualified.

Create an ignored `*.private.yaml` configuration derived from the example. Set both `transmit_enabled` and `enrollment_enabled` to `true`. In `esphome.build_flags`, supply `-DX2D_TRIAL_IDENTITY_SUFFIX=VALUE` with your privately verified byte value (0…255). Enrollment builds still require this explicit definition. The profile initializes a **new journal**; its suffix is then persistent and remains authoritative across firmware changes, including for subsequent additions. Changing the build flag does not rewrite the stored profile: a mismatch refuses additions and retries with `association_profile_unqualified` before identity allocation or RF. Commands-only builds need no suffix flag and can still use stored bindings when their compiled profile differs.

Remove `X2D_TRIAL_SLOT` and `X2D_TRIAL_EXPECTED_NEXT_COUNTER`: they are now rejected at compile time, including in commands-only builds. They cannot restrict a lifecycle controller that owns slot allocation and durable attempt claims. The lowest unused slot is selected for a new addition. With a pending candidate, **Associer un volet** requests the single explicit 2/3 retry after a complete 0/1 attempt. Partial attempts and further retries refuse transmission. No startup, reconnect or OTA recovery restores an emission permit. Confirmation targets the pending physical slot, after both counters have been reserved and while radio work is idle, and always means the human observed the motor response.

Do not publish the private configuration or binaries. Supervise every addition/retry; this build no longer provides a per-slot compile gate. There are no arbitrary seeds, counter resets or automatic retries. This adapter provides no controls for cancelling an incomplete candidate, replacing or retiring a shutter, changing service state, or initializing a legacy v1 journal. Such states remain durable and cannot be cleared through its two buttons. A legacy journal reports `initialization_required`; interrupted v2 initialization finalizes through the core before RF admission. Do not erase or restore a journal to work around an unsupported state.

A commands-only build uses `transmit_enabled: true` and `enrollment_enabled: false` with already confirmed identities, keeping association disabled. The reference example and CI builds keep RF disabled.

## Required measurements

Record board/module/motor models, firmware and core SHAs, wiring, supply, clock calibration, capture equipment and the raw captures. Use only disposable test motors and supervised movements.

1. **Boot and faults:** capture GDO0 and RF during power-up, reset, brownout and watchdog while transmitting. Verify the external pull-down suppresses the carrier and measure the maximum time to carrier-off. Verify boot issues CC1101 reset/IDLE and never emits a command.
2. **Waveform:** compare RMT captures against core-generated chips and an observed remote/known transmitter. Check polarity, chip duration, one preamble, copy continuity and phase, footer and full final chip, including odd run counts. Verify repeated idle-to-transmit transitions preserve the GPIO matrix route and produce a waveform on GDO0. Recheck after chip calibration.
3. **Interrupt latency:** exercise Wi-Fi/API traffic, logging and NVS writes during a burst. Inject ISR stalls shorter and longer than a half-buffer. The refill monitor has a 1 ms margin, a 1 ms early/5 ms late completion tolerance and a 50 ms completion watchdog; these software thresholds need bench validation. Every injected missed deadline must return `unknown` and latch RF off. Detection cannot undo emitted stale symbols.
4. **STOP:** issue STOP at different offsets under load. Capture accepted STOP to the previous burst's last chip, then to the STOP burst's first chip. Record p99 and maximum latency and verify each normal truncation ends at a full, not-yet-prefetched frame. Do not advertise the RP2040's one-frame latency or treat network button latency as a hardware bound.
5. **Association:** use new private identities, verify the initial 0/1 attempt and only deliberately test 2/3 retry. Confirm the motor's actual response before pressing Confirm. Power-cut before/after both reservations and between phases. Verify no counter reuse, no restart/reconnect emission, and confirmation after reboot without retransmission.
6. **Movement and malformed transmissions:** verify open, close and STOP on the target motor. On a sacrificial motor, capture the behavior of truncated or half-repeated frames generated by injected faults. Unexpected actuation blocks qualification.
7. **Home Assistant and OTA:** confirm stable entity IDs and custom names through another association/reboot. Check pending and unused slots remain absent. Interrupt application OTA while RF is active and while idle: first flash write must follow radio quiescence, counters remain consumed, failed OTA never replays work. Verify the journal partition bytes and inventory survive a successful app update.

Do not enable normal association or announce compatibility when any gate fails. Record the failed criterion and keep RF disabled outside supervised private trials. Software lifecycle support does not replace motor evidence.
